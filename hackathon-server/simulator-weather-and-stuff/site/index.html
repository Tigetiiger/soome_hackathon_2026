Compactor weight without a scale

Waste compactors already know their fill level, how often they press and how hard the ram pushes. We simulate a fleet of press containers hour by hour and train a hydraulic soft sensor that turns those signals (plus public weather) into an estimate of the mass inside, every hour. The value just before emptying lets a customer check the transporter's weighbridge figure without installing a load cell.

All data is synthetic. Nothing here represents real Europress data or pressure settings.

Model run shown: models/full400 (trained on runs/full400, 400 machines). Chosen by default: models/full400/metrics.json is newer than runs/full400 data. Simulator sections show runs/full400.

T_day(r,d) = (Jan_r + Jul_r)/2 + (Jul_r - Jan_r)/2 * season(d)  + sd(d) * a(r,d)

hourly T   = interpolated daily mean + diurnal cosine (amp 1.5–4.5 °C, max at 15:00)
             + AR(1) noise (sd 0.4, phi 0.9), re-centred to the exact daily mean
hourly rain: block of 2.0 + 1.5*sqrt(mm) + Exp(1) hours, Dirichlet(0.7) weights
snow       : precipitation while T < 0.0 °C; snowpack melts 3.0 mm per °C-day
yard wet   : store = store * exp(-1/6.0 h) + rain + melt;  index = min(1, store / 2.0 mm)
indoor RH  : outdoor vapour pressure at 21.0 °C (Magnus) + 8.0 %, clipped 15–65 %

Why: weather is the only public input that changes how heavy a load is at the same volume (wet fibre), and frost and cold oil change the pressure signal; it has to look like Finnish weather.

3 · Material inflow

lambda(h)  = daily_input / mean_event_mass * hour_weight(sector,h) * day_factor * month_factor * open(h)
             * daily_noise(d) * bulk(d)                    (normalised so the year averages to daily_input)
events(h)  ~ Poisson(lambda(h))
mass(h)    ~ Gamma(events * 2.0, mean_event_mass / 2.0)   (sum of gamma event masses)
daily_noise: AR(1) lognormal, phi 0.6, sigma_log 0.3
bulk(d)    = x U2.0–4.0 on 0.02 of open days, else 1
presses(h) ~ Binomial(events, 0.85) + Poisson(0.004 test cycles per open hour)

Mean event mass: cardboard 18 kg, paper 22 kg, plastic_film 9 kg, mixed_waste 12 kg. Holidays (Finnish public holidays incl. Easter, Ascension, Midsummer, All Saints) close every sector except hotels.

Why: press counts and timing are real machine signals; tying them to loading events makes "presses since lift" a genuine proxy for the number of loads.

4 · Moisture pathways

Each pathway is tracked as its own truth column (true_water_humidity_kg, true_water_carried_kg, true_water_hopper_kg, true_free_water_kg, true_ice_kg), so the effect of each can be checked separately.

humidity  EMC% = 5.8 * (h/(1-h))^0.3   (Oswin, h = RH/100 <= 0.97)
          storage air = 24 h EMA of outdoor / indoor RH, mixed by the site's outdoor-storage share
          extra = hygroscopic * (EMC - 7.0) / (100 + 7.0)     hygroscopic: cardboard 1.0, paper 1.0, plastic_film 0.0, mixed_waste 0.3
carry-in  water = mass * carry_in_frac * yard_wetness   (outdoor infeeds only)   carry_in_frac: cardboard 0.04, paper 0.035, plastic_film 0.01, mixed_waste 0.02
          absorbed share cardboard 1.0, paper 1.0, plastic_film 0.1, mixed_waste 0.5; the rest runs off as free water
hopper    rain/snow kg = mm * aperture area * ingress  (open 1.0, lidded 0.1, shaft 0.0), pressed in with the next stroke
          soaked up by the pack: cardboard 0.3, paper 0.3, plastic_film 0.1, mixed_waste 0.4
free water: drainage -> drained;  standard container -> seeps out (tau 120 h);  sealed -> pools
          free water and ice add mass, not volume
frost     below 0.0 °C (container temp = 12 h EMA of air): free water and hopper snow -> ice,
          melts at 0.02/h per °C; wet loads freeze into lumps (+cardboard 0.15, paper 0.12, plastic_film 0.08, mixed_waste 0.15 volume), thaw tau 12 h
wet density: rho x (1 + coeff * moisture_extra), coeff cardboard 0.6, paper 0.6, plastic_film 0.0, mixed_waste 0.2, extra clipped -0.1–0.3

Why: moisture is the reason a fill-based estimate drifts with the weather: water adds kilograms without adding volume and makes fibre pack denser and softer.

5 · Compaction & container state

layers   one per loading day since the last lift (max 40); each stores base mass, water by pathway,
         reference volume vref, frost volume and pmax = the highest pack pressure it has seen
density  rho = rho_typ * machine_factor * daily_factor * (1 + a*moisture_extra) * g(pmax)
         g(P) = (1 + c*ln(1 + P/20.0)) / (1 + c*ln(1 + 120.0/20.0))      (log-law, plastic: a layer only gets denser)
         machine_factor: lognormal cv 0.15 * (force/30 t)^0.15;  daily_factor cv 0.07
         compressibility c: cardboard 0.25, paper 0.15, plastic_film 0.35, mixed_waste 0.12
springback right after a stroke the pack is cardboard 0.04, paper 0.02, plastic_film 0.08, mixed_waste 0.02 denser, relaxes with tau 3.0 h
fill     = sum(layer volume) / container volume;  material that does not fit -> side waste (true fill never > 100 %)
chamber  loads in an hour without a stroke wait in the press chamber and go in with the next stroke

The mass ledger start + material in + hopper precipitation − drained − seeped − side waste − lifted = end is checked per machine.

Why: the hidden per-machine density factor and the daily scatter are exactly what a fill reading cannot see and what the pressure has to reveal.

6 · Hydraulics & the pressure signal (centrepiece)

The ram pressure is the only signal that sees the pack. Its law follows what a Europress specialist told us in the expert interview (parameter values are assumed — real ones are unknown):

expert saidsimulator
back pressure is visible from 5–10 % fill, then it rises until fullfill_onset 0.07, fill_exponent 1.0 (linear)
the ram enters only 20–30 cm into the containerpressure graph rises only in the last part of the stroke (ram_ingress_frac 0.2)
fill tells volume; weight is a question markmedium density dependence, density_exponent 1.0
single strokes spike — a box crushed on the way inspikes +20–80 bar with probability cardboard 0.06, paper 0.02, plastic_film 0.01, mixed_waste 0.03 per stroke
the machine can log the pressure graph16 samples per stroke → per-stroke curve features
f(fill)  = clip((fill - 0.07) / (1 - 0.07), 0, 1)^1.0
demand   = k * f(fill) * stiffness * (rho/rho_typ)^1.0 * soft(moisture) * (1 + 1.5*frost_share)
           k = 150.0 bar (machine cv 0.05), stiffness cardboard 1.0, paper 1.1, plastic_film 0.85, mixed_waste 0.9
           soft = clip(1 - wet_softening*moisture_extra, 0.3, 1.5), wet_softening cardboard 2.5, paper 2.5, plastic_film 0.0, mixed_waste 0.5
P_stroke = min(relief, visc(T_oil) * (P_idle + demand * resistance_noise)) + crushed-item spike
           P_idle U20–30 bar, relief 240.0 bar (cv 0.02), resistance cv 0.05
visc     = 1 + 0.004 * max(40.0 - T_oil, 0)       (cold oil reads high)
T_oil    : +0.8 °C per stroke, cools to ambient with tau 2.0 h, max 60.0 °C
P_pack   = min(demand, relief/visc - P_idle)    -> the layer's pmax; density growth stalls once the valve opens

The pressure graph (per stroke)

x        = ram position, 0 = stroke start, 1 = end; 16 samples
x_c      = 1 - 0.2 - 0.15 * clip((fill - 0.07)/(1 - 0.07), 0, 1)  +/- 0.03   (contact point)
P(x)     = min(relief, visc * (P_idle + excess * ramp(x)^q + loose(x))) + spike bump
ramp     = clip((x - x_c)/(1 - x_c), 0, 1);  q = 1.2 + 2.0*(0.35 - c);  loose material up to 8.0 bar before contact
device   : contact_pos  = first x where P rises > 15.0 bar above the start   (where the pack face is)
           rise_slope   = (P_end - P_contact) / (1 - contact_pos)                 (stiffer / denser pack rises faster)
           mean_bar     = mean pressure over the stroke                             (proportional to hydraulic work)
           completion   = share of the travel done before the relief valve opened

The end-of-stroke value equals the stroke peak, so the curve is consistent with peak_pressure_bar. Hourly means of the four device features become the curve_* columns; raw curves are kept for the first 5 machines.

Why: pressure is what separates a light from a heavy container at the same fill; making its dependence on density only medium keeps the problem honest — as the expert said, weight is a question mark.

7 · Sensors

The fill level is not measured directly. With fill_mode: pressure_derived the firmware estimates it from the back pressure, like a real Europress-style machine without a separate level sensor:

P_avg   = exponential rolling average over ~10 strokes (alpha = 2/(n+1) per stroke), reset at a lift
fill %  = 100 * clip((P_avg - P_idle) / P_full, 0, 1)        no oil-temperature correction (compensate_oil_temp: False)
P_full  = 150.0 bar * material stiffness, calibrated per_installation (machine scale, idle, typical density)
spurious high: 0.0167 per day, 1–4 days at 88–97 %, only once true fill >= 15.0 %, clears at lift
dropouts: 0.01 of hours + outages 0.02/day, duration lognormal(mu, sigma = 1.8, 0.8) h (max 96 h)
pressure transducer: noise 4.0 bar, 0.5 bar steps, gain bias sd 0.01, drift sd 0.02/yr

So cold oil reads fuller, day-to-day density scatter and spikes leak into the reading, and the README documents ≈ 6 pp mean error for this setup (the interview said about 5 %).

Measured on this run (runs/full400, 3,442,827 hourly readings): mean |fill_pct − true fill| = 6.0 pp overall, 5.8 pp at true fill ≥ 30 % (includes the spurious-high episodes).

Why: the model's main input is itself an estimate derived from pressure; pretending it is a clean level sensor would overstate what fill alone can do.

8 · Operations

order    when the observed fill >= threshold (80/85/90 %), or 30 h before the max interval of 28 days
truck    arrives 4–30 h after the order, only 6–18 h local, no Sundays: True, no holidays: True
lift     COMBI: press + container lifted and emptied fully; DUO: container swapped, residue U0.0–0.02 of the layers
         the final stroke before the swap also takes the chamber contents
weighbridge  net kg with resolution 20 kg and noise sd 15 kg

The lift happens at the start of the lift_event hour; the row one hour earlier holds the mass that was lifted — that is the value compared with the weighbridge.

Why: false-full readings and truck delays make the container's fill at lift vary widely, which is what the soft sensor has to get right.

Explicitly excluded inputs

The simulator also writes the truth. None of it may reach the model, or the test numbers would be meaningless (leakage). Excluded are every column starting with true_ — in runs/full400: true_mass_kg, true_fill_pct, true_density_kg_m3, true_moisture_extra, true_water_humidity_kg, true_water_carried_kg, true_water_hopper_kg, true_free_water_kg, true_ice_kg, true_false_full — and the FORBIDDEN_COLUMNS:

How the AI built this

The simulator, the training pipeline and this page were written by an AI coding assistant working from the hackathon brief. Below is its own account of the work.

Covers: 1. The idea · 2. The simulator (compactor_sim) — done and validated · 3. First ML evidence (simulator baseline, before the recalibration) · 4. The training pipeline (weight_train) — in progress

Hydraulic soft sensor — what we built with AI, and the math behind it

Europress × Since AI Hackathon 2026 (Turku). All data is synthetic; nothing represents real Europress data.

1. The idea

Europress compactors already report fill level, press count and ram pressure. A soft sensor turns those signals (plus weather) into an estimate of the mass of waste in the container every hour — no scale needed — so a customer can check the weight the transporter reports at the weighbridge. The number that matters most is the estimate just before each lift.

There was no real data, so the work has two parts:

  1. compactor_sim — a physics-motivated simulator producing realistic hourly telemetry and the true mass (ground truth).
  2. weight_train — the machine-learning pipeline that learns mass from telemetry only (in progress).

How the AI was used: Claude (Claude Code) designed the module layout, wrote the simulator, tests, validation and baseline scripts, ran and calibrated them, and used an independent AI reviewer agent to audit the core code. Background agents handled side tasks (documentation refresh) in parallel.

---

2. The simulator (compactor_sim) — done and validated

  • 400 machines × 365 days, hourly: 3.5 M rows + 5.7 M individual ram strokes, ~30 s on a laptop.
  • All parameters in config.yaml; same seed ⇒ bit-identical output.
  • 18 pytest tests pass; per-machine mass balance closes to ~1e-9 kg.

2.1 Weather (5 Finnish regions, south → north)

  • Temperature: seasonal sine + AR(1) anomaly, partly shared between regions T(d) = T̄ + A·cos(2π(d − d_warm)/365) + σ(d)·x_d, x_d = φ·x_{d−1} + √(1−φ²)·ε_d
  • Precipitation: two-state Markov chain for wet/dry days (p(wet|dry) = π(1−r), p(wet|wet) = π(1−r) + r, persistence r = 0.35, keeps the wet-day share π), gamma-distributed wet-day amounts, spread over showery hours with Dirichlet weights.
  • Snow below 0 °C; snowpack melts with a degree-day model (3 mm per °C per day).
  • Indoor humidity from outdoor vapour pressure (Magnus formula) e_sat(T) = 6.112·exp(17.62·T / (243.12 + T)) evaluated at 21 °C → dry indoor air in winter.

2.2 Moisture — four pathways, each kept as its own truth column

pathwaymath
humidity → fibreOswin isotherm MC% = 5.8·(h/(1−h))^0.30 (7 % at 65 % RH, 14 % at 95 % RH), 24 h memory
wet material carried ina few % of mass × yard wetness index (rain/melt store with 6 h decay)
rain into the hopperkg water = rain_mm × hopper_area_m² (1 mm on 1 m² = 1 kg); lid lets in 10 %
free waterfibre soaks 30 % (plastics 10 %); rest drains, seeps or pools; freezes below 0 °C

Wet fibre compacts denser: ρ × (1 + 0.6·moisture_extra). Free water adds mass, not volume.

2.3 Compaction (one layer per loading day)

  • Log-law: ρ = ρ_typ · machine_factor · daily_factor · g(P_max) with g(P) = (1 + c·ln(1 + P/P₀)) / (1 + c·ln(1 + P_ref/P₀)) (c = 0.25 cardboard, 0.15 paper, 0.35 film, 0.30 mixed plastic).
  • Each layer remembers the highest pressure it felt (compaction is plastic).
  • Springback: after a stroke the pack relaxes 2–8 % over ~3 h.
  • fill = Σ layer volume / container volume; what doesn't fit is side waste.

2.4 Hydraulics

  • P_peak = min(relief, visc(T_oil) · (P_idle + k · f(fill) · stiffness · (ρ/ρ_typ)^1.5 · softening · frost))
  • f(fill) = clip((fill − 0.3)/0.7, 0, 1)^1.6 — at low fill the ram barely touches the pack.
  • Cold oil reads higher: visc = 1 + 0.004·max(40 − T_oil, 0); oil warms 0.8 °C per stroke.
  • The relief valve (~240 bar) caps pressure — the pack stops densifying when it opens.

2.5 Sensors and operations

  • Fill sensor: 3 % noise, gain bias ±3 % with drift, false-full bridging episodes (reads 88–97 % for 1–4 days), 1 % dropouts + outages.
  • Pressure transducer: 4 bar noise, 0.5 bar steps, ~2 %/yr drift.
  • Order at observed fill ≥ 80/85/90 %; truck 4–30 h later, only 06–18, no Sundays/holidays.

2.6 Validation highlights

  • Lift masses inside target ranges for cardboard, paper and film; mixed plastic slightly above — the brief itself conflicts (DUO-only, ≥ 20 m³, 115 kg/m³ ⇒ ≥ ~1.9 t when full).
  • Pressure separates densities at high fill (correlation 0.77–0.94).
  • Weather effect: open-hopper cardboard ~4–6 % heavier after a wet week; indoor shaft ~0 %.
  • ~6 % of lifts are triggered by false-full readings (those containers are light).

2.7 AI code review → fixes

An independent AI reviewer found no significant correctness bugs, but showed that overflow was the normal case (44 % of lifts had side waste). We recalibrated the median time to the order threshold from 4.5 to 8 days (still within the brief's 2–10 days), cutting side waste from 9.7 % to ~3.5 % of inflow, and fixed two small labelling issues.

4. The training pipeline (weight_train) — in progress

  • No leakage: only device-known columns are whitelisted; true_* columns and hidden machine parameters are forbidden; today's daily weather totals are excluded (they contain future hours).
  • Features (one function for training and prediction, backward-looking only): hours since lift, forward-filled fill, max fill since lift, 24 h fill change, presses since lift, pressure statistics, rain for each of the last 7 days, rain × infeed exposure, frost days since lift, machine type, hour and weekday.
  • Physics-based pressure inversion (fitted per material on training rows only): log(P/visc − P_idle) = log k + a·log(fill) + b·log(ρ), inverted to an implied density ρ̂. The fitted exponent b ≈ 1.4–1.6 recovers the simulator's 1.5 without ever seeing it.
  • Target: kg per m³ of container, multiplied back by volume (one model for all sizes).
  • Two evaluation splits: unseen machines (25 % held out, tested on the last 25 % of the period) and same machines in later months; each with separate validation and calibration slices.
  • Models: shared model vs per-material models, the better one kept per material; small tuning grid; monotonic-constraint check.
  • Prediction interval: p10/p90 quantile models + conformal calibration (normalised CQR, stratified by material × fill bucket) targeting 80 % coverage.
  • Status: an early test fit on the same-machines split reached ~3.4 % mean error per lift (pre-recalibration data). Plots, REPORT.md, tests and the full 400-machine training run are next.
