1Overview and methods

This document describes a hydraulic soft sensor that estimates the mass of waste inside a press-container compactor every hour, using only signals the machine already produces — the firmware fill reading, press counts, peak ram pressure, the per-stroke pressure graph and oil temperature — together with public weather data. Gradient-boosted tree models (LightGBM) are trained on a physics-based simulation of 400 machines over one year. Evaluated in the hour before each lift against the true container mass, the selected model reaches a mean error of 3.7 % on the same machines in later months and 5.3 % on machines it has never seen, against 32.1 % for the naive fill × density estimate. A calibrated p10–p90 interval accompanies every estimate. Sections 2–4 document the model, Section 5 the simulator that generated the data, Sections 6–8 limitations, downloads and a glossary.

All data in this document is synthetic. It was generated by the simulator described in Section 5. No number here is a measurement from a real Europress machine, and the hydraulic parameters of the simulator are assumptions, not real Europress settings. The results demonstrate the method, not field performance.

1.1What is estimated

The quantity of interest is the mass of waste in the container, in kg, at the end of every hour. The model predicts mass per m³ of container (the kg/m³ target) and multiplies back by the container volume, so one model covers all container sizes. The number a customer cares about is the estimate in the pre-lift hour: the last hour before the container is emptied, which is the mass the transporter's weighbridge later reports. Every hourly estimate comes with a p10–p90 interval.

The machine does not have a level sensor. Its fill reading is a firmware estimate derived from a rolling average of back pressure, and it says little about weight: the same fill can hold a light or a heavy pack. The peak pressure, the shape of the pressure graph along the stroke and the number of presses since the last lift carry the information about density that the fill reading lacks; weather matters through the moisture of fibre materials and through cold hydraulic oil.

1.3Evaluation protocol

Two splits are reported. Same machines, later months is the pilot-fleet case: every machine is in the training data, and the model is tested on the later part of the year. Unseen machines is the new-installation case: a quarter of the machines, stratified by material, are held out entirely and tested on the later part of the year. In both splits the validation slice (tuning, early stopping, shared versus per-material choice) and the calibration slice (interval width) are taken from rows that are neither trained on nor tested; in the unseen-machines split they are other held-out machines. The first, incomplete cycle of every machine is excluded everywhere.

1.4Metrics and notation

Unless stated otherwise, a metric is computed over the pre-lift hours of the test split (one value per lift) and compares the model estimate with the true container mass of the simulator. 'Hourly' metrics use every test hour instead. Percentage errors are relative to the true mass and exclude lifts below 200 kg, where a percentage is meaningless (near-empty false-full lifts). Table cells under 5 % — the hackathon target for the mean error at lift — are shaded in the result tables.

2.4Pressure-implied density

The feature rho_p turns a stroke's peak pressure into a pack density by inverting a simple pressure law fitted on training rows only. The density used for fitting is derived from the training label (kg/m³ divided by the fill fraction), never from simulator truth columns.

P / visc(T_oil) − 25 bar = k · (fill/100)^a · ρ^b          (per material; log-linear least squares)
visc(T_oil) = 1 + 0.004 · max(40 − T_oil, 0)
used only when fill > 25 % and P < 228 bar (below the relief valve), ρ clipped to 20–900 kg/m³
rho_p_smooth = EWMA of rho_p within the cycle (half-life 4);  pressure_kg_m3 = fill/100 × rho_p_smooth

The fitted exponent b is close to 1 for every material, i.e. the pressure depends about linearly on density — the 'medium' dependence built into the simulator — and the fill exponent a is near 1 as well.

5.7Sensors

The fill level is not measured directly. With fill_mode pressure_derived the firmware estimates it from the back pressure, like a real Europress-style machine without a separate level sensor:

P_avg   = exponential rolling average over ~10 strokes (alpha = 2/(n+1) per stroke), reset at a lift
fill %  = 100 * clip((P_avg - P_idle) / P_full, 0, 1)        no oil-temperature correction (compensate_oil_temp: False)
P_full  = 150.0 bar * material stiffness, calibrated per_installation (machine scale, idle, typical density)
spurious high: 0.0167 per day, 1–4 days at 88–97 %, only once true fill >= 15.0 %, clears at lift
dropouts: 0.01 of hours + outages 0.02/day, duration lognormal(mu, sigma = 1.8, 0.8) h (max 96 h)
pressure transducer: noise 4.0 bar, 0.5 bar steps, gain bias sd 0.01, drift sd 0.02/yr

So cold oil reads fuller, and day-to-day density scatter and crushed-item spikes leak into the reading.

Measured on the 400-machine run (3,442,827 hourly readings): mean |fill_pct − true fill| = 6.0 pp overall, 5.8 pp at true fill ≥ 30 % (including the spurious-high episodes). The expert quoted about 5 %.

Relevance for the model: the model's main input is itself an estimate derived from pressure; pretending it is a clean level sensor would overstate what fill alone can do.

6Limitations and caveats

6.1Synthetic data and assumed parameters

Every number in this document comes from simulated data. The simulator (Section 5) was built from the hackathon brief, public product information and one expert interview; many of its parameters are marked 'assumed' and none of its hydraulic settings are real Europress values. Results therefore show what the method can do when the physics behaves as modelled, not what it will do in the field. A real pilot with weighbridge tickets is needed before any of the error figures can be trusted.

6.2Known issues in the generated data

  • Heaviest lifts above 10 t. 34 of 20,281 lifts removed more than 10 t (maximum 11.7 t net), all mixed_waste in 20 m³ DUO (34). A hook-lift truck carries about 10 t, so these lifts would not happen in reality; mixed waste at 400 kg/m³ should be restricted to containers of at most 14–17 m³. The soft sensor is unaffected (it predicts kg/m³), but the mixed-waste mass range of the data is wider than a real fleet's.
  • One synthetic weather year. The data cover a single year (October 2025 to September 2026) of one weather draw. Annual precipitation of that draw: LAP 359 mm (target 490), PIR 601 mm (target 620), POP 526 mm (target 520), UUS 723 mm (target 660), VAR 722 mm (target 670). A single year has an interannual spread of about 12 %, like real Finnish data; the generator is unbiased over seeds, but the model has seen only this one year, and there is no microclimate, wind-driven rain or roof overhang.
  • Uneven coverage of materials and container sizes. cardboard: 200 machines on 10, 14, 17, 20, 22, 24, 27, 30, 35 m³; mixed_waste: 40 machines on 10, 14, 20 m³; paper: 60 machines on 10, 14 m³; plastic_film: 100 machines on 10, 14, 17, 20 m³. Paper and mixed waste are rare and appear on few container sizes, so the unseen-machines results for them rest on small test sets and the model has not been tested on sizes outside this mix.
  • The fill reading is less accurate than quoted. The simulated firmware estimate is 6.0 pp from the true fill on average (5.8 pp above 30 % fill), against the roughly 5 % the expert quoted. This is a property of the simulated calibration (per-installation setpoint, no oil-temperature compensation) and makes the fill features somewhat weaker than on a real machine if the quote is right.
  • The contact point of the pressure graph is only weakly informative. Its correlation with the true fill is -0.34 at fill ≥ 35 %, because the ram enters only 20–30 cm into the container (ingress fraction 0.2) and loose material blurs the onset. The rise slope, not the contact point, is what the models use (Section 2.5).

6.3Caveats of the model and its evaluation

  • Interval coverage of mixed waste on unseen machines is 31 % against the 80 % target (hourly: 56 %). Only 100 test machines are held out in that split and mixed waste is 10 % of the fleet, so the calibration slice for this material is small, and its error scale does not transfer to the test machines. All other material × split cells are within a few points of the target.
  • The test period is one season. Both splits test on 2026-07-01 to 2026-09-30: summer and early autumn. Winter behaviour (frost, ice, cold oil, snow in the hopper) is in the training data but has not been tested on held-out data.
  • Evaluation is against the true mass, not against a weighbridge reading. A real comparison adds the weighbridge's own error (in the simulator 20 kg steps and 15 kg noise, about 0.5 % of a typical lift) and, for DUO, the uncertainty of the tare.
  • Small negative bias. The estimate at lift is on average -24 kg (same machines, later months) and -54 kg (unseen machines) below the true mass. A plausible reason, not verified here, is that the fullest hour of a cycle is where the pressure saturates at the relief valve and where the training data are thinnest.
  • False-full episodes hurt. 10.2 % of all lifts are triggered by a spurious-high fill reading; lifts whose cycle contained such an episode have a mean error of 5.5 % versus 3.5 % without (same machines, later months; 7.2 % versus 5.0 % on unseen machines).
  • The first cycle of every machine is excluded from training and testing because it is incomplete; a freshly installed machine therefore has no estimate until its first lift, and the features that count 'since lift' are undefined before it.
  • The pressure graph and the per-stroke log are assumed to be available for every machine. On the simulated fleet the hourly curve features exist for all machines; raw curves are kept for five. If a real machine logs only the hourly peak pressure, the ladder shows what is lost (Section 3.2).
  • Hyper-parameters come from a four-point grid and a single training seed. The spread of the learning curve over machine draws (Sections 3.6 and 4) is the only variance estimate reported.
  • The reference load cells on 5 % of the simulated machines are not used; a real deployment would use them to calibrate and monitor the soft sensor.

6.4Simulator limitations

Things the simulator does not model, which could change the picture on real machines:

  • Weather is regional (5 points), without microclimate, wind-driven rain, roof overhangs or hopper canopies.
  • The pressure law is phenomenological and its parameters are not real Europress values. There is no position or stroke-length signal, no partial strokes and no pack friction along the container.
  • All layers share the pack pressure of each stroke: no pressure gradient front-to-back, no creep beyond springback.
  • Each machine is its own site. Co-located machines and shared staff behaviour are not modelled; side waste leaves the system instead of being reloaded after the lift.
  • Hygroscopic moisture is fixed at loading; the closed container does not exchange moisture afterwards, and pooled water does not soak into the bottom layers.
  • Truck logistics are simple (independent random delay, no route capacity). Lifts during telemetry outages still happen.
  • The true-fill ceiling differs slightly by material because of springback (about 93–98 % right after a stroke).
  • With the default cycle lengths no site reaches the 28-day maximum interval, so scheduled lifts of part-full containers are almost absent from the data.
