## B. Hooklift and roller-container standards and load transfer

**DIN 30722** (roller contact tipper vehicles and roller containers; SEC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roller_container; DIN listings found via search)
- Hook directed 45° upward, with the handle bar at **1,570 mm** height.
- Roller wheels: **1,560 mm inner / 2,160 mm outer** spacing.
- Lengths in 250 mm steps from 4,000 to 7,000 mm.
- Height is not standardised.

| DIN 30722 part | Scope | Max mass |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1 | Roller tipper vehicles | 26 t |
| Part 2 | Hooklift | 32 t |
| Part 3 | 900 mm system | 16 t |

- The standard text itself is paywalled and was not read. Wikipedia notes that DIN "concentrates on the hook ... and the wheels".
- The container slides or rolls on steel rollers. Wikipedia notes steel rollers damage asphalt and that concrete is preferred.

**SS 3021:2014** (Swedish dimensions standard for hooklift frames; https://www.sis.se/en/produkter/road-vehicles-engineering/commercial-vehicles/trucks-and-trailers/ss30212014/, TECH listing)
- Valid, edition 5. JOAB says Swedish deliveries follow SS 3021 (https://www.joab.se/manuals/manuals/JOAB_WEB_MANUALS/en-gb/Content/HookLifts_EcoDrive/About_The_Hook_Lift.htm, MFR).
- A container listing cites a 1,450 mm hook height "SIS 3021" (SEC).
- Hook heights of 900, 1,000 and 1,450 mm are also on the market.

**SFS 4519:** **not found.** No Finnish standard with that number could be identified. Finnish hook-height practice is **not found**. The CombiMax drawing's country-specific wheel allowances ("Fin, Den, Nor 30, Swe 80") suggest Nordic variants (INF).

**Ground contact and load transfer**
- **Not found in a standard text.**
- From BACE patent US11162834B2 (PAT, see D): containers have "two parallel longitudinal main rails" and wheel sets. The patent weighs them by guiding the rails onto load-cell rollers so that the wheels lift off the ground.
- **INF:** a roller container on the ground typically bears on its rear rollers and its front rail ends or skids. The load is shared between those contact zones in proportion to the centre-of-gravity position.
- Contents shift the centre of gravity as the ram packs waste toward the rear. So **measuring only one end (for example, two rear sensors) gives a CoG-dependent fraction of the mass**. Canalytics (US12379240B1) "extrapolates" total weight from two scales at the hopper end for exactly this reason.

## D. Patents

**US11162834B2, "Weight-measurement retrofitting for waste compactors"** (BACE LLC; inventors F. Waite, D. Sigmund Jr.)
- Filed 2019-04-01; granted 2021-11-02; priority claimed to 2012-11-30. https://patents.google.com/patent/US11162834B2/en (PAT)

*Claim 1*
- A guide frame with two parallel longitudinal guide surfaces sits laterally beside the container's two main rails, so the rails are guided as the container rolls over the frame.
- At least two scale devices on the frame extend laterally outward. Their weight sensors are spaced to match the rail spacing.
- In the use position the rails rise onto the sensors, which then **bear the full weight** of the container and its waste.

*Claim 15*
- A ground frame plus a container with two wheel sets.
- The sensors are cylindrical load cells with transverse axes forming a curved ramp that lifts the rails, so the wheels leave the ground.

*Claim 19 (method)*
- Position the frame, align the container, and drive it so the guides direct the rails onto the scales until the scales alone support it.

*Dependent claims*
- Tapered self-aligning entry, rotating sleeves (roller assemblies), **load-pin cells as roller axles**, ramps, and communication to a remote computer.

*Method*
- Sum all load-cell readings, then subtract the known empty container weight looked up by type and size (or average and subtract an averaged tare).
- The remote computer issues weight-based notifications.

*Citations and cited-by*
- Cited-by includes WM Intellectual Property "Smart baler" patents (US11780190B2, US11850818B2, US12138883B2, US12269233B2) and US12070921B1 "Baler with internal scale".

## E. Literature

### E.1 Onboard weighing: Radoičić, Jovanović, Arsić (2016)
- "Experience with an On-board Weighing System Solution for Heavy Vehicles", ETRI Journal 38(4):787–797, doi:10.4218/etrij.16.0115.0183 (PR). Abstract: https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201671261180471.page
- **Scope:** experimental study on a refuse collection vehicle with a hydraulic rotating drum. It measured:
  - container kinematics
  - load-manipulator dynamics
  - vehicle-mass vibrations
  - stress in the supporting structure
- **Method:** proposes a "universal measurement system" for the force on the rear axle (curb weight plus payload). It also proposes an alternative on-board system for continuous weighing while moving during collection.
- Keywords include strain gauge, piezoelectric, transducer. HBM and Kistler data sheets are referenced.
- **Measured accuracy: not found.** The full text could not be retrieved (403 / PDF over 10 MB).

**Known error sources** (from the patents and snippets above)
- Friction in cylinder seals
- Hydraulic losses between the sensor and the ram
- Oil temperature and viscosity
- Short sampling windows
- Uneven ground
- Cylinder wear

**Implication (INF):** inferring mass from *compactor* ram pressure is a harder problem than loader weighing. The ram pushes waste horizontally against the container and friction rather than lifting it. Pressure therefore reflects compaction resistance, not gravity.

### E.3 Dynamic weighing compensation
- R51 approvals describe static mode (stop and wait for a stable reading) and dynamic mode (weigh while moving) (sik.dk MID certificates, TECH).
- US20120285750A1 uses an accumulator damper plus averaging, with up/down differencing (PAT).
- Hiab integrates force over the full stroke (PAT).

### E.4 Strain gauges on structure
- **Not found:** no peer-reviewed accuracy figure for strain gauges bonded to a container or compactor frame.
- HBK guidance (F) implies that force shunts through stiff structure, side loads and thermal strain are the dominant errors.

### E.6 Compaction mechanics (stress–density)
- **Not found:** no fitted power-law stress–density model for waste paper or OCC was found in this pass.
- Related MSW geotechnical work (not read in full; SEC):
  - Zhang et al. 2016 (WIT Trans.): strain grows logarithmically with vertical stress.
  - Zeng et al. 2019: ln(void ratio) is linear in pressure.
  - Zekkos et al. 2017 (J. Geotech. Geoenviron. Eng.): 143 one-dimensional compression tests; compressibility parameters estimated from composition and unit weight.
  - Bareither, Benson & Edil, ASCE 2012: immediate compression of MSW.
- **INF:** these support a log-type relation, ρ ≈ ρ₀ + k·ln(σ/σ₀), rather than a clean power law. Parameters must be fitted per fraction.
- Voicu 2023 shows that per-stroke force on cardboard is highly random. That argues against single-stroke pressure as a mass predictor, and for accumulating over many strokes.

## F. HBK load-application guidance
Source: https://www.hbm.com/tw/3377/load-application-in-load-cells/ (MFR)

- "Correct load application on the load cells is a prerequisite for precise weighing results."
- Loads outside the measuring axis cause errors and shorten life. **Avoid side forces, bending and torsional moments.**
- Compression cells need **level, rigid supports**. Uneven settling tilts the structure, shifts load distribution and creates lateral forces; supports should lower by the same amount.
- **Thermal expansion:** rigid mounting converts horizontal movement into horizontal forces "frequently sufficient to distort the measurement result".
- **Elastomer bearings:** up to 15 mm horizontal travel, up to 800 N restoring force, 1.7° tilt. They shorten about 1 mm at full capacity, which can cause significant errors.
- **Self-centring pendulum cells** (for example C16A, 20–400 t): max 13 mm / 5° deflection. Pendulum bearings tolerate about 3° without significant error.
- **Load introduction:**
  - Rocker-pin loading feet (HLC) compensate movement and temperature.
  - Fixed bearings restrain movement but can distort the signal; calibrating with defined weights reduces this.
  - Rocker bearings distort least but restrain weakly.
  - Knuckle eyes suit quasi-static tension; flexible tension bars suit dynamic loads.
  - Cones and conical pans suit high-accuracy and legal-for-trade use, but not vibration.
- **Stops and stay rods:** use stops (angle stops, rubber pads) and anti-lift-off devices. Stay rods must be aligned, because even slight tilt causes friction and **vertical force shunts**.
- **Number of supports (three vs four):** **not covered** on this page.
- **Implication (INF):**
  - A compactor that is pushed horizontally by its own ram, and lifted on and off by hooklift, creates exactly the large side loads and shocks HBK warns about.
  - Sensors therefore need decoupling elements (pendulum or rocker), overload and lift-off stops, and preferably a measurement taken with the ram at rest.
