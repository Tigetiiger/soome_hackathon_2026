# Shared brief — Europress × Since AI Hackathon 2026 (Turku, 6–8 Nov 2026)

Read this first. Team of ~4 students, 48 h. Goal: win the Europress challenge **"SMART Material Value Engine — Weigh at Source, Create Value"**.

## Official brief (Europress Group Oy, contact Tommi Häkkinen, CIO)
**Part 1 — GET THE WEIGHT:** obtain sufficiently accurate weight data directly at the compactor with an *affordable, scalable* solution, integrated or retrofitted. Explain sensor choice, placement, installation, calibration, limitations, cost and a **validation experiment**. No live-machine integration or physical prototype required. Compare feasible options (load cells, structure/strain measurement, hydraulic signals, or a justified alternative).
**Part 2 — CREATE VALUE FROM THE WEIGHT:** combine weight with SMART + commercial data to create measurable customer value and new commercial services. Demonstrate >=1 concrete use case (EUR/t optimisation, material-value transparency, anomaly detection, recommended actions) and consider other services, business models, revenue. Compare AI vs transparent rule-based logic; consider explainability, uncertainty, **human approval** of actions.

**Scoring:** 25% weighing concept (feasibility, accuracy reasoning, cost, retrofit) · 25% customer/business value · 20% data-driven value concept & analytical reasoning (traceable calculations) · 15% working software demo + SMART integration pathway · 10% commercial productisation · 5% clarity, uncertainty, limitations.

**Submission:** (1) short illustrated weighing concept: principle, placement, indicative BOM + cost, installation, calibration, error sources, validation plan; (2) working demo or recorded walkthrough on synthetic data, code/setup notes, traceable economics, proposed SMART integration; (3) concise customer value + business case: use case, measurable customer benefit, commercial model for Europress, how to productise inside SMART. **List every third-party library/model/dataset/service with licence and commercial-use restrictions.** State assumptions and what remains untested.

## What Tommi said in the kick-off (transcript analysis + his handwritten notes)
- Europress: ~EUR 70M revenue, aiming to double; HQ Kerava FI + factory in Poland (Poland = key future market). Leading Nordic compactor/baler maker. Mission: promote recycling.
- Machines (all IoT/"intelligent" for ~15 years): COMBI (compactor + container welded, whole unit lifted) and DUO (compactor stationary, container detached). Balers too (100–500 kg bales).
- **Fill level** is computed from hydraulic ram **back-pressure** (0–200 bar, time series per stroke), ~5% accuracy. Also run hours, e-stop, faults. Customers are very price-sensitive: **no new sensors on the bill of materials unless nearly free ("the winning solution is the cheapest")**; accuracy need only be ballpark ("not by the kilo", maybe +-10-12%).
- Today: 60–70% of FI pickups are automatic "on call" at ~80% fill; rest fixed schedule. Poland/Central Europe still mostly traditional fixed schedules. SMART already tries to make machines as full as possible before pickup -> *do not pitch "smarter pickups" as the core; SMART does that*.
- **Core pain: the waste operator alone holds the weight (weighbridge ticket, truck in/out)** and the material-quality grade; customers distrust invoices; operators don't always share data. "Who owns the data is the holy grail." Operators (esp. the biggest in Finland, which owns trucks) earn per transport -> conflict with fuller containers.
- Europress will NOT operate trucks / enter waste business. It's a technology/manufacturing company: wants to "know the weight, ballpark", be earlier in the value chain, become a service provider (subscription).
- Business model today: sell machines (customers mostly lease, ~6-year leases) + service contract ("the real profit"). SMART tiers: **Intro / Insights / Integrated**. SMART is only ~15% of customers: big retail chains want more SMART features, small customers want to save money (80/20, "medium and big fries analogy"). Insights = optimisation + subscription. Predictive maintenance (ML on pressure data) just launching.
- Confounders for weight: material type (bio-waste hardest/liquid; cardboard & plastic easy), humidity/rain changes cardboard weight (e.g. +100 kg over a weekend), contamination / wrong waste in the wrong container (recognising this = a stated second problem), container geometry. Customers usually have 2-3 compactors: mixed, cardboard, bio. Recycling rate: ~70% recycled in the chain per Tommi; Finland ~50%. Customers are "not educated enough" (education opportunity).
- Europress has tried things before but won't say what. IP: whatever we build should be ownable by Europress; licensable third-party tech allowed if licence permits (must list licences).
- He wants to see **creative use of AI** in the work itself, and business-model ideas ("what would we do with the data?", resell data to others, "how can they change the whole industry").
- Idea list from his notes: (1) predictions: when will it fill; (2) dashboard: machine health; (3) dashboard + readability of data for the customer, amount of data processed, prediction from raw data, automatic decision-making from extracted info. Problem 2: recognise material that doesn't belong. Note: "we do not have the sensor data, we are predicting, not rationalising the raw data => reverse-engineering?" (i.e. the hackathon dataset has NO pressure curves; only fill, mass, telemetry events).
