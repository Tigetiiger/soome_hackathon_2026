Weight add-ons for Europress SMART

Challenge: "SMART Material Value Engine — Weigh at Source, Create Value". This page explains our business plan so all four of us can pitch it without notes. Read it once top to bottom: problem → solution → money → risks. Dotted-underlined words link to the glossary.

  1. Europress already sells weighing. Scale Solutions 4-corner platform (a scale under the machine, built from load cells), Scaletipper X1, a "waste volume calculator", SMART tiers, SMART Center, an operator API and Retrofit kits F. We propose upgrades to these, not a new business.
  2. Weigh. Turn the volume calculator into a pressure-based virtual scale that reports kg with an uncertainty band (e.g. "11.8 t ± 0.7"). €0 BOM (no extra hardware) on every SMART machine. Operator tickets calibrate it (correct its reading); the existing scale platforms supply ground truth (trusted reference weights).
  3. Create value. Europress becomes the neutral second opinion on what the operator weighs and invoices: Verified Weight, Proof of Fullness, a €/t benchmark and a reporting pack. The system drafts every action; a human approves it.
  4. Money. Add-on fees inside Insights / Integrated and the lease / service contract. As software alone it is small. The real return is more SMART customers and more service-contract renewals, where Europress makes its profit T.
  5. Sequence. Start with the 20% who hold the value (multi-site chains). Free kg teaser for the other 80%. Use Proof of Fullness to open Poland.

Say it like this

The 30-second pitch

Europress compactors already know how full they are. With a firmware upgrade they can also estimate how heavy they are, for €0 of extra hardware. Operator tickets keep that estimate honest, and Europress's own scale platforms supply ground truth. Inside SMART, that weight becomes a neutral second opinion:

  • every month is reconciled against the tickets and the invoice;
  • differences are explained by cause, without blame;
  • fixed-schedule customers get proof of which pickups they didn't need;
  • AI drafts every recommended action, and a person approves it.

It is sold as an add-on to the existing SMART tiers, the lease and the service contract. Small customers get a free kg number; chains and fixed-schedule sites pay from the value they measure. The result: more customers on SMART, stickier service contracts, and Europress earlier in the waste value chain, without owning a single truck.

Problem · 1

Who owns the weight in the value chain?

The customer pays by the tonne, but only the waste operator ever weighs the waste. Europress's machine sits at the very start of the chain and already measures how full it is. It just does not report kilograms.

material (waste) money (invoice) data where kg is measured
  1. Customer site (retail chain, logistics, property) owns or leases 2–3 compactors per site, pays the invoice, has no independent weight.
  2. Europress compactor with SMART knows fill level and ram back-pressure; today it reports no kg. With add-ons it reports kg with an uncertainty band.
  3. Operator truck lifts the container, may carry side waste or combined loads, earns per transport.
  4. Operator weighbridge weighs gross minus tare equals net; the only measured kg today; issues the ticket.
  5. The invoice flows back to the customer: transport plus €/t processing minus material credit, based on the operator's kg.
  6. Data: the compactor sends fill, pressure and faults to the SMART cloud. With add-ons the cloud sends kg and a €/t check to the customer, and receives operator tickets when shared.

Solution · weigh (Part 1)

How does a compactor know kilograms?

Push and measureEach ram stroke (compaction: the press squeezes the waste) pushes it into the container. The hydraulics already measure the back-pressure (0–200 bar) T.
Pressure → density → kgPressure × strokes × fill level → how dense the load is → kg. A formula from physics gives the first guess; a machine-learning model (software that learns from examples) corrects the rest.
Tickets set the gainClean tickets (weighed tare, single load) tell each machine how far off it reads, and keep it honest over time.
Be honest about the evidence. The hackathon dataset has no pressure curves T. Our accuracy numbers come from literature E; the dataset's mass_kg is a simulated future sensor. The cheapest proof is Gate 0: a ≈€25k desk test on real pressure archives (roadmap).

What the dataset's weights are for: reference answers

The synthetic dataset contains two kinds of weight for each lift: the device's estimated kg and the weighbridge ticket (truck weighed full on the way in and empty on the way out). The tickets act as reference answers (ground truth, or "labels"): the right answers we check our model against, like an answer key for an exam.

  • (a) Train and calibrate the virtual-scale model: learn how readings map to kg, per machine.
  • (b) Measure how wrong it is: the error bands on the ladder below.
  • (c) Detect drift: spot a machine whose gap to the tickets slowly grows.
  • (d) Prove value: find billing gaps, unneeded lifts and €/t differences in money terms.

Limits. The dataset has no raw pressure curves and is synthetic, so it cannot prove real-world hardware accuracy. That is what the pilot with the scale-platform golden cohort is for: real machines, real reference weights.

The accuracy ladder: what each rung costs and how precise it is

Each rung reuses something Europress already has. Every band sits on the same ±20% axis around the true weight. Solid = the optimistic end of the range; hatched = out to the pessimistic end.

Why monthly totals are tighter, and why that is not enough on its own

One machine, one month: 7 lifts of ~1.69 t. Each dot is one device reading with its ±12% whisker; the dashed line is the true weight. Some read high, some low.

random noise on the sum: 12% ÷ √7 ≈ 4.5%
uncalibrated (bias 10%): √(4.5² + 10²) ≈ 11% → 11.8 t ± 1.3
after ~20 clean tickets (bias 3.4%): √(4.5² + 3.4²) ≈ 5.7% → 11.8 t ± 0.7
Averaging kills noise, not bias. That is what T1 is for. Random errors partly cancel when you add 7 lifts. A machine that reads 10% heavy reads 10% heavy every time, so the month is still 10% off. Clean tickets measure that bias and remove it; CUSUM watches for it creeping back (drift).
Solution · weigh, what it is good for

One lift lies, a quarter tells the truth

Chance of catching a systematic invoice inflation (we accept flagging 1 honest invoice in 40 by mistake; robust median of clean ticket/device pairs) D. Two sensors: the dataset's simulated precise sensor (a future scenario, σ≈1.4%) and Tommi's ballpark σ = 10%.

Fig 3 · Detection power vs aggregation window · D parametric sweep on real lift counts · σ10 at 10 months not computed (hollow point).

The lesson: per lift, a ballpark scale proves almost nothing. Aggregated over a quarter or a contract year, it catches systematic overbilling. So we verify at contract level, not per lift, and use robust medians (plain sums fail: sd 36%, because side-waste and tare outliers dominate).

Solution · critical stance

Operator-neutral: verified service quality for everyone

Why this matters. Europress's new CEO (Petri Salermo, from Oct 2025) and incoming CCO (Eljas Tuovinen, Jan 2027) both come from environmental services at Lassila & Tikanoja, a major Finnish operator F. Operators are also Europress customers and public references F.

So we never say "catching operators". We say:

  • Cause attribution first. Most gaps are tare and allocation, not intent (77% vs 3.9%) D.
  • Operator sees findings about its own vehicles first.
  • Give-to-get: operators that share tickets get a free tare-hygiene report.
  • Tendering or switching is the last action, and it is gated.
  • Differences go both ways: a wrong stored tare can also make the customer lose credit.

Action ladder (escalates only with approval)

1. Explain the gap by causedefault
2. Share with operator firstoperator-first

Words we must use correctly

Glossary

Lift
One collection: the truck lifts/empties the container. Our unit of analysis.
Unjustified lift
A lift made while the container was not full enough to need it (in the dataset, labelled by fill at lift). Mostly on fixed schedules.
Gross / tare / net
Weighbridge weighs the loaded truck (gross) and the empty truck (tare). Net = gross − tare = the waste weight billed.
Stored tare
A saved empty-truck weight reused instead of weighing the truck empty each time. Goes stale (fuel, equipment, mud) → systematic error. 30% of big gaps D.
Weighed tare
Truck actually weighed empty on that trip. More accurate (robust SD 3.8% vs 7.5% stored) D.
Weighbridge ticket
The operator's record of gross, tare and net for a trip. Operators share these only sometimes. A clean ticket has a weighed tare and a single load.
Side waste / combined load
Other waste on the same truck, or several containers in one trip split 50/50. Both distort per-container weight.
On-call vs fixed schedule
On-call: SMART calls the truck at ~80% fill (60–70% of Finnish pickups). Fixed: truck comes on a calendar whether full or not (most of Poland/CE). Hybrid: a mix.
€/t
Euros per tonne: processing price per tonne of waste. Comparing €/t across contracts is what exposes tariff gaps.
Mass-billed €
Invoice lines whose amount is computed from a weight (€/t × tonnes), as opposed to fixed transport fees.
COMBI / DUO
COMBI: compactor and container welded, whole unit lifted. DUO: compactor stays, container detaches.
Compaction / ram stroke
The compactor's hydraulic ram (a big piston) pushes waste into the container to squeeze it; each push is one stroke.
Back-pressure (hydraulic)
Hydraulic ram pressure (0–200 bar) per compaction stroke. Already used for fill level; we use it to estimate kg. Not in the hackathon dataset.
BOM
Bill of materials: hardware cost per unit. T0/T1 add €0.
OTA
Over-the-air firmware update. T0 needs one to log pressure at 10–20 Hz.
SMART Intro / Insights / Integrated
Europress's digital tiers (contents and prices not public). Our reading E: entry, optimisation + subscription, and full integration (APIs, ERP).
SMART Center
Europress remote support team; up to 90% of problems fixed remotely F. Would approve our AI drafts.
Load cell
A metal block that bends very slightly under weight and turns that into an electrical signal: the sensor inside most scales. T2 uses four of them.
Scale Solutions / Scaletipper X1
Existing Europress weighing: 4-corner load-cell platform under the compactor; bin tipper with 1.0 kg resolution.
Waste volume calculator
Existing Europress tool estimating weight from average container weights, without a scale. T0 upgrades it.
Retrofit / SMART Retrofit
Adding a kit to a machine that is already installed (any brand) so it connects to SMART, instead of buying a new machine.
T0 / T1 / T2
Our accuracy ladder: virtual scale, ticket-calibrated, measured (load cells).
Golden cohort
Small set of machines (2–5% of fleet) fitted with T2 scales to provide ground truth.
Ground truth / reference weight / label
The "right answer" a model is trained and tested against: here a weighbridge ticket or a scale-platform weight. The model's error is measured against it.
Synthetic data
Data generated by a computer simulation to look realistic. Good for building and testing methods; it cannot prove how real hardware performs.
Kalman / CUSUM
Kalman filter: a standard method that updates a machine's calibration a little each time a clean ticket arrives. CUSUM (cumulative sum): adds up small differences over time and raises an alarm when they keep pointing the same way, which catches slow drift.
Drift
A machine's reading slowly moving away from the truth (wear, sensor ageing). Looks like bias that grows over weeks.
Bland-Altman
Standard method to measure agreement (bias and limits) between two measurement methods.
Leave-one-machine-out
Test the model on a machine it was not trained on, for each machine in turn. Shows how it does on a new customer.
Ablation
Remove one ingredient (here: some of the tickets) and see how much accuracy drops.
p10 / p50 / p90
Percentiles. p90: 9 in 10 lifts have an error at or below this value. p50 is the median (middle value); p10 is the low end.
σ / 1σ / robust SD
σ (sigma, standard deviation) is the typical size of an error. About 2 in 3 readings fall within ±1σ of the truth. Robust SD is computed so outliers like side waste do not inflate it.
± band (uncertainty band)
"11.8 t ± 0.7" means our best guess is 11.8 t and the true weight is very likely between 11.1 and 12.5 t. A wider band = less sure.
Bias vs random error
Random error: readings scatter high and low, so they cancel out when you add many lifts. Bias: a machine reads, say, 10% heavy every time; adding lifts does not cancel it. Calibration removes bias.
Calibration / anchor
Correcting a machine's reading by comparing it with a trusted weight (an "anchor", e.g. a clean weighbridge ticket). Each anchor tunes that machine's gain factor.
z-score
How unusual a value is, counted in σ: z = 3 means 3 typical errors away from normal. Used to flag outliers.
Mean / median / robust median
Mean = average. Median = the middle value when sorted; one extreme value cannot drag it. A robust median uses this to ignore outliers like side waste.
Macro-F1
A 0–1 score for how well a method sorts cases into the right category (1 = perfect), averaged so rare categories count as much as common ones. Benchmark: rules 0.73, ML 0.35, rules + ML 0.78 D.
Best action set / identified value
For each contract, the most valuable combination of actions (renegotiate, tender, dispute, request data) the dataset's valuation rules allow. €1,064 per contract-year on average D.
Realisable value
The part of identified value a customer actually captures. We assume 50% A.
Cost to serve
What it costs Europress per device per month to run the add-on: cloud, ML, AI drafting, support. €3.5 assumed A.
Contribution
Revenue minus cost to serve, before fixed development costs.
Proof of Fullness
Fill + tonnage evidence that a scheduled lift wasn't needed.
Gain-share
Fee = a share (25%) of the verified saving instead of a fixed subscription.
Give-to-get
Operators share tickets and get a free reconciliation receipt and hygiene report back.
BDO
Poland's national waste register (database), where companies record waste they produce and hand over; reporting waste mass is mandatory F.
CSRD / ESRS E5
CSRD: EU law making large companies publish sustainability reports. ESRS are its reporting standards; ESRS E5 covers resource use and circular economy (E5-5: outflows incl. waste). The Omnibus narrowed CSRD to large companies F.
NAWI 2014/31/EU / legal-for-trade
EU directive on non-automatic weighing instruments. A scale whose weight is used to bill must be certified ("legal-for-trade"). Ours is a second opinion only.
EN 643
European standard for recovered paper grades; >10% moisture can be claimed back.
EU Data Act
EU rules on access to and use of data from connected products; needs consent clauses.
k-anonymous
Aggregates published only when at least k (here 5) customers are in each cell.
Device-year
One device active for one year; in the dataset each device has one contract, so = contract-year.
NPV / payback
NPV (net present value): all future cash in and out, converted to today's money (future € count less; here 6 yrs at 10% a year). Positive = worth doing. Payback: the year the cumulative cash turns positive.
ARR
Annual recurring revenue: the subscription money that comes in every year.
Gross margin / break-even
Gross margin: share of revenue left after the direct cost of serving it. Break-even: the size at which revenue covers all costs, including fixed development.
API / CloudEvents
API: a machine-to-machine data connection (here SMART ↔ operator systems). CloudEvents: a standard message format for sending such events, e.g. "lift weighed".
Attach rate
Share of customers/machines that take SMART (or an add-on). Today ~15%.
