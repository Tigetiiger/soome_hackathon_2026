Reconciliation: device · ticket · invoice

Every lift with a device mass and a weighbridge ticket is checked against an expected band, 2 × √(device σ² + ticket σ²). The ticket σ comes from the data for its tare method: stored tares scatter about twice as much as weighed ones.

What could make this wrong

The dataset's device weight is a simulated integrated scale (σ 1.4%). A real T0 virtual scale is about 9× noisier per lift, so fewer gaps are "outside the band" and more need a look. The accuracy switch above applies the T0/T1 σ to the same lifts.

Causes of large gaps

Large gap = device and ticket differ by more than 10%. Causes come from the dataset's labels and the observable ticket fields (tare method, loads on vehicle, driver-reported side waste). These are weighbridge-hygiene causes, not intent.

What could make this wrong

The stored-tare effect might be a simulator artefact. Each vehicle's offset is identical in both halves of the window, which is what a stale stored value looks like, but real fleets change equipment and fuel load. Findings go to the operator first, as a private hygiene report.

Proof of Fullness

SMART already fills on-call containers well, so this is not a pickup optimiser. It is evidence for customers on fixed schedules (common in Poland and Central Europe) that a lower frequency or SMART on-call would do. Fill shows the lift was not needed; the compactor's kg is the tonnage evidence.

SMART Center · approval lane

The single exit of Verified Weight: every proposal waits here for a person. Approve, hold (ask for evidence) or reject; hold and reject need a reason. Each proposal names the rule that created it, cites the source rows, shows its value as a p10/p50/p90 range with its evidence tag, and states how to reverse it. Approving writes a CloudEvent to the outbox; in production SMART would deliver it. Here nothing leaves the page. Proposals are pre-computed on the whole dataset, so the filters do not hide them.

    Method & limits: how the weight is obtained
    
    
    Three accuracy levels on things Europress already sells. Ballpark is enough for most decisions, so the default costs €0 in hardware. All accuracy figures are 1σ estimates [E] until the pilot measures them.
    
    
    The dataset's weights are our reference, not proof of hardware
    
      
    The provided dataset's weights (the device estimates and the weighbridge tickets) are used as the reference (ground truth) to build, calibrate and evaluate everything here: the ticket σ per tare method, the bands, rule R1, the cause attribution and the calibration simulator. Because the dataset is synthetic and has no raw pressure curves, it cannot prove what a real compactor's pressure-based weight will achieve. That proof comes from the pilot's golden cohort: real machines on scale platforms, weighed against weighed-tare tickets (see the validation plan below).

    How the virtual scale works: pressure → force → compaction work → mass
    Model, untested
    
      
    
        
    1 PressureExisting 0–200 bar transducer on the ram circuit. New firmware logs P(t) per stroke at 10–20 Hz: peak, hold, rise rate, stroke time.P(t)
    
        
    2 ForceRam area times pressure, minus seal friction (depends on oil temperature). E.g. 100 mm bore at 200 bar ≈ 157 kN [A].F = P·A − Ffric(Toil)
    
        
    3 Compaction workAt constant pump flow, stroke time is a position proxy, so work per stroke is the integral of force over travel.W = ∫ F dx
    
        
    4 Charge massSpecific compaction work per kg from a density law fitted per material (Walker/Heckel type), with springback from the hold-pressure decay.Δm = W / wmat(σ)
    
        
    5 Container massSum since the last emptying (which re-zeroes it), times a per-machine gain; cross-checked with fill × volume × density and combined by inverse variance.m = gdev · ΣΔm
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Sensor placement and installation
      
          
    • No new sensor for T0/T1. The existing pressure transducer and PLC; firmware adds curve logging at 10–20 Hz, pushed over the air. No site visit.
      • 
          
    • Oil temperature: proxy from the time of an unloaded stroke, or an optional €10–25 probe [E].
      • 
          
    • Zero: the container is empty after every emptying, so each emptying re-zeroes the sum for free.
      • 
          
    • T2: 4 load cells under the container platform (or cradle); 3–5 h with 2 technicians, easiest as a factory option or at a service visit that already happens.
    
        
    Calibration scheme
      
          
    • Commissioning: fleet prior from the golden cohort, zero at first emptying, optional one reference weigh (€100–250 trip if no tickets) [A].
      • 
          
    • Running: every clean ticket updates the machine gain with a robust (Student-t) Kalman step; gain changes above 8% are flagged; CUSUM opens a drift watch.
      • 
          
    • T2: dead-weight check with certified masses at install, auto-tare at each emptying, checks on routine technician visits.
      • 
          
    • Honest result: on the dataset a naive gain correction made per-lift error worse (3.0% → 5.3% with 1–5 tickets) and a rolling correction left it unchanged (2.8% → 2.8%). Tickets fix bias and drift, not noise, which is why T1 is a monthly claim.

    Validation experiment and acceptance
      
        
    • Desk test (2 weeks): SMART pressure archive, ≥30 machines × ≥10 clean tickets; leave-device-out; pressure features must beat fill-only by ≥30% on median error, or the concept stops.
      • 
        
    • Golden rig (4–6 weeks): 6–10 machines on platforms, ~150–240 lifts with weighed-tare tickets; one-day bench test of 40 charges of known mass; rain, snow and a cold start included.
      • 
        
    • Analysis: Bland-Altman on the log ratio, leave-one-machine-out, ticket-count ablation k = 0/3/5/10/20, conformal 80% bands.
      • 
        
    • Accept if: monthly error ≤6%; per-lift p90 ≤20% (= ±12% at 1σ); 80% band covers 75–85% of lifts; ≥30% better than fill-only; T2 ≥95% of lifts within ±3.3%.
      • 
        
    • Falsified if: pressure adds <10–15% over fill-only, gain wanders >10% month to month, wet-load bias >8% cannot be modelled. Fallback: fill + anchors (~9% median error on synthetic data) or sell the platform.
    
      
    Limits: what we do not claim
      
        
    • All data is synthetic. The dataset's device weight is a simulated integrated scale (σ 1.4% + 15 kg), better than T0 would be. Its press_load_index is derived from fill (r = 0.985), so the dataset cannot validate pressure → mass.
      • 
        
    • No peer-reviewed study shows mass-from-pressure on compactors; the weighbridge reference itself is only ±40–80 kg (OIML R76).
      • 
        
    • Wet streams are harder: on single loads without side waste, 78% of bio-waste lifts are within ±10% of the ticket vs 88% for cardboard. Bands are widened there, or those sites move to T2.
      • 
        
    • All € are hypothetical tariffs, not savings. Decision values are simulated expectations, not causal evidence.
      • 
        
    • Contamination and moisture detection does not work on this data: our density proxy reaches ~1% precision (). Roadmap, not a feature.
      • 
        
    • The stored-tare effect may be a simulator artefact; only a pilot can confirm it.
      • 
        
    • Verification, not billing. Billing on device weight would fall under NAWI 2014/31/EU legal metrology.
      • 
        
    • Poland: BDO reporting and Polish ticket formats are untested.
      • 
        
    • No pickup routing or timing. SMART already does that.
      • 
        
    • Patents: found by title only (e.g. US8794135B1, US11162834B2); no freedom-to-operate check yet.

    AI-assisted build
    
      
    
        
    To build this
    Claude Code agents did the data-value analysis, the weighing and literature review, a red-team "judge" pass, the build architecture, build_data.py and this module. Every number shown is recomputed from the dataset by build_data.py or in the browser; review agents audited claims against the parquet files, and the team decides what ships.
    
        
    In the product: rules decide
    Rule R1 (invoice mass vs two independent masses) flags 25 lines; all 25 carry the dataset's weight-basis-mismatch label (precision 100%, recall 66% of labelled lines with a ticket) and never sees labels. In the dataset's own Task B benchmark, readable rules score 0.73 macro-F1 vs 0.35 for gradient boosting (hybrid 0.78). We did not run an LLM-as-classifier bake-off: no API key in the build environment.
    
        
    Where an LLM helps: drafts
    An LLM may only draft the letter text from the cited rows. A guard rejects a draft that cites a row outside the evidence, uses a number not in the evidence (±0.5%), or uses blame words; then the rule template is used. One pre-written sample draft, clearly labelled, is shown in proposal P-001 with the guard running live in your browser. No model runs on this page.
